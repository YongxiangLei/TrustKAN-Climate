\begin{abstract}
Reliable climate forecasting requires more than low point-prediction error: a model should also communicate uncertainty, remain informative under distribution shift, and indicate when its predictions should not be trusted. This paper develops \method, an interpretable and reliability-aware forecasting framework built around temporal Kolmogorov--Arnold Network (KAN) representations. The framework combines intrinsic nonlinear functional mappings with probabilistic prediction, conformal calibration, distribution-shift scoring, and selective prediction. A dedicated calibration split is used to avoid tuning reliability thresholds on the final test data.
