\section{Conclusion}

We built a trustworthy climate forecasting framework in the form the literature encourages, pre-registered the tests it would have to pass, and it failed almost all of them. \method{} wins none of forty seed-matched paired comparisons on the Central England Temperature record; the budget-matched plain KAN that was included specifically to isolate the temporal KAN module is significantly better at every horizon beyond one day, with the gap widening monotonically with lead time. The fused reliability score is beaten by its own interval-width component at every horizon and ranks high-error forecasts at chance, so the abstention rule built on it recovers one to three percent of error where discarding half the origins should recover far more. Conformal marginal coverage is accurate, but split conformal guarantees that for any backbone, and the joint coverage a practitioner would depend on falls to $0.103$ at ninety days.

The ablation is more damaging than the benchmark. Substituting a budget-matched multilayer perceptron for the KAN mapping moves RMSE by at most $0.008$\,\degC{} at any horizon, inside the across-seed spread, so the component that gives the architecture its name and its claim to interpretability is functionally interchangeable with the baseline it was meant to improve upon. Nor does that component earn its keep as an explanation: retrained runs that reproduce their ledger RMSE exactly show that a given univariate curve corresponds to nothing stable across seeds, and the two statistics that appear to say otherwise are matched exactly by controls that carry no information. Of five intended contributions, four are withdrawn on their own pre-specified ablations.

Two mechanisms survive, and both belong to the conformal wrapper rather than to anything we designed. Selective forecasting driven by interval width alone improves on no abstention by up to \widthGainNinety{}, with the benefit growing as forecasts get harder. Adaptive conformal calibration reduces rolling coverage deviation by up to \adaptiveDevDropNinety{} for a width cost under \adaptiveWidthCostThirty{}, and its advantage also grows with lead time, which is where coverage stability is hardest to hold. A practitioner wanting the reliability behaviour this paper set out to deliver should take the wrapper and discard the rest.

The pre-registered corruption sweep refutes the last standing claim and, in doing so, explains the others. \method{} degrades worse than both comparators at every corruption and level, and its error stops responding entirely once a masked block passes three days. Measuring the effective receptive field directly confirms why: of the \robustHistory{} days of history the model is configured to receive, \robustFieldTrust{} reach its readout, against the full window for both comparators. The architecture is not underperforming its design so much as failing to implement it, and a configured context length turns out to be no evidence of an effective one.

The study's methodology did the work its architecture could not. Freezing the protocol, binding artifacts to checksums, and writing claims against required evidence in advance is what turned a result that would have looked publishable into a refutation, and is also what surfaced a silent architectural defect that a trivial baseline comparison exposed and a code review had not. The receptive-field limit came the same way, from a pre-registered corruption level behaving impossibly rather than from inspection.

The wider point concerns how reliability is credited. A system that contains conformal calibration, a shift detector, and an abstention rule is not thereby reliable, and evaluating such a system as a whole hides which parts contribute. Measured against its own pre-specified ablations, each component of our design either failed or was surpassed by something simpler, while the one number that looked best belonged to a wrapper that any model could wear. Reliability machinery should be evidenced component by component, against the ablation that would remove it, and reported when that evidence does not arrive. The same holds for temporal context: it costs one perturbation loop and no training to check that a model reads the history it is given, and a comparison between architectures with different effective windows is not the comparison it appears to be.

Future work should test whether these conclusions extend beyond a single station, using the station-generalization protocol already frozen here, and should revisit the reliability score with fusion weights selected on the validation partition rather than fixed by assumption, under a separately pre-registered plan.
