\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What Is Claimed and What Is Not}
The accuracy result is parity. On the pre-specified family the corrected architecture is statistically indistinguishable from the strongest baseline, and we have written the tables so that a reader cannot mistake a favourable mean for a verdict: the ranking table says in its own caption that bold marks a point estimate, and the paired table prints the inconclusive count beside the wins. What the comparisons establish is narrower and, for a reliability argument, more useful than a leaderboard position. Each corrected element is charged for its own contribution, and the contributions land where the mechanism says they should: the wide stem and the attention readout are each necessary, the readout matters only at the long leads, and the design is significantly better than both the ablation that removes its temporal module and the published architecture it replaces.

That framing is deliberate. A reliability contribution that depended on also winning an accuracy contest would be a weaker contribution, because it would be untestable wherever the accuracy contest went the other way. The diagnostics reported here apply to a model that ties, a model that wins, and a model that loses.

\subsection{Configured Context Is Not Effective Context}
The receptive-field result is the finding we would most like other groups to reuse. A look-back window is reported as a property of a model because it is a hyperparameter of the experiment, but what a forecast can depend on is a property of the layer composition, and the two can differ by two orders of magnitude with no symptom that a standard evaluation would surface. In our published design the gap was \robustFieldPublished{} steps against \robustHistory{}. Training converged, marginal coverage held, and short-horizon accuracy was competitive, which is exactly what a model with a three-day view should produce on a temperature series.

What exposed it was not code review but a corruption level behaving impossibly: error that stopped responding when more of the input was destroyed. A metric that cannot move is a stronger signal than a metric that moves the wrong way. We would encourage two habits on the strength of this. First, report the effective receptive field for any architecture whose contribution is framed in terms of temporal context; it costs one perturbation loop and no training, and it should be measured on the reported weights rather than on a fresh initialization, with the reproduction gate that makes that claim checkable. Second, treat saturation anywhere in a corruption sweep as a structural signal rather than as a plateau, because a bit-identical response to a larger perturbation has only one explanation.

For reliability engineering specifically, the diagnostic addresses a hazard that coverage and error-detection metrics cannot: a model can be perfectly calibrated over a distribution it is not actually reading. Interval width would still track difficulty, abstention would still function, and every reliability number in this paper would still be attainable, while the system silently ignored the input a domain expert believed it was conditioning on.

\subsection{Freezing a Procedure Rather Than a Constant}
The fusion result generalizes a point about pre-registration. Freezing a constant is normally treated as the conservative option, because it forecloses tuning against the test set. It is not conservative when the constant governs how evidence is combined. An equal weighting gives half the mass to a component that may be uninformative, or whose association with error reverses with lead time, and the fused score can then rank worse than its better input --- a failure with no upside, since the fused score is offered as an improvement on that input.

Selecting the weight on the calibration partition, over a grid whose endpoints are the single-component scores, removes that failure by construction rather than by experiment: both components are candidates, so the selected fusion cannot lose to either on the data used to select it. The property that matters more in practice is the second one. The grid lets the data withhold mass from a component that does not earn it, up to withholding all of it, which turns the combination rule from an assumption into a measurement. On CET the procedure placed \fusionWeightLow{}--\fusionWeightHigh{} of the mass on interval width and never chose the equal weighting in \fusionRuns{} runs.

We are explicit about how much that bought. Fused selective risk is close to width-only selective risk at every horizon, so the shift term's marginal contribution remains negligible on this record even after fitting. The gain is not a better score; it is that the method reaches the correct conclusion about its own components from calibration data, rather than requiring a separate study to refute the weighting. The distinction is the one between a design that is right and a design that can find out.

\subsection{Where the Reliability Signal Works and Where It Does Not}
Error-detection AUROC rises from \errorAurocOne{} at one day to \errorAurocNinety{} at ninety. The low end is not noise and should not be presented as one: at one day the score is at chance. We take this to be a property of the forecasting problem rather than of the score. Day-ahead temperature error on a single station is dominated by atmospheric variability that no signal internal to the model can anticipate, and a confidence score that appeared to predict it would warrant suspicion rather than confidence. The useful reading for a reliability engineer is that the signal becomes informative precisely where the forecast becomes hard, which is where an escalation or abstention policy has something to act on, and that a deployment should not expect self-assessment to help at leads where the residual is irreducible.

The joint-coverage results carry a similar shape. Marginal coverage lands on nominal and means little, because split conformal delivers it for any predictor; per-origin joint coverage falls to \jointCoverageNinety{} at ninety days. The simultaneous construction is the one that supports a trajectory-level decision, and it holds at \simulCoverageThirty{}--\simulCoverageNinety{} for a width cost of \widthRatioThirty{} to \widthRatioNinety{} times the marginal band. Reporting marginal coverage alone, which is common, would overstate trajectory reliability by a wide margin.

\subsection{Interpretability Remains Refuted}
The KAN layer's univariate curves are individually unstable across seeds, their apparent stability is an artifact of a narrow shape space, and they are mostly near-linear. This was true of the published architecture and remains true of the corrected one, and we report it unchanged. Two aspects are worth keeping. The controls are what make the measurement decisive, and they are cheap: pairing curves at random, and matching each curve to the most similar other curve within the same model, both reproduce the flattering statistics almost exactly. Stability analyses of learned univariate functions are typically published without such controls. And the correction does remove one objection: the curves are now functions of latent channels computed from the whole window rather than from \robustFieldPublished{} days, so what they describe is at least the right domain. That makes them worth inspecting in principle. It does not make them reproducible.

\subsection{Relation to Our Earlier Report}
An earlier version of this work reported the same framework as a component-wise refutation, and that record stands. The claim--evidence matrix and the pre-registration in this repository retain the refuted rows, the two mid-study defect disclosures, and the campaigns that produced them. Nothing here is offered as a correction of the earlier reading of that evidence: under the published stem and the frozen weighting, the conclusions drawn were the conclusions the evidence supported. What changed is the design, and the two changes were each prompted by a diagnostic that the earlier study produced. We think the sequence is the argument. A pre-registration that only ever confirms is not doing work; one that refutes, localizes a mechanism, and then licenses a corrected design is.

The cost of that discipline is visible in this study too. Because the provenance hash covers the whole source tree, changing the model source invalidated every completed run and forced re-execution of the entire campaign; the audit refuses to aggregate across fingerprints precisely so that the cost cannot be avoided by mixing old results with new. One consequence deserves its own note. The classical baselines are quoted in Table~\ref{tab:cet_rmse} from the superseded campaign, because refitting them costs about twenty-five hours, almost all of it in one hyperparameter search. That is only sound if the source edit cannot reach them, so we established rather than assumed it: every architecture shared by the two campaigns reproduces its stored test predictions byte for byte under the changed source, and the imported rows carry their own fingerprint in the caption and in the provenance record.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
The evidence is a single long station record. The station-generalization campaign that would test breadth is specified, frozen, and unexecuted, and we expect it to be the first thing a reader asks for; nothing in this paper establishes that either diagnostic behaves the same way across stations, though neither is specific to a dataset. The corruption sweep covers the pre-specified comparison family rather than every baseline. Conformal guarantees depend on exchangeability assumptions that temporal dependence and nonstationarity strain, which is one reason joint coverage degrades. Mahalanobis-type embedding distance captures only one form of representation shift, so the shift component's small contribution is evidence against this operationalization rather than against shift-aware reliability as an idea. The fusion grid is one-dimensional because there are two components; with more components the selection would need a search strategy and a multiplicity argument that we have not developed. Selective prediction is only meaningful where abstention or escalation is operationally available. And the correction costs several times the training time of the cheapest baseline, which is reported in Table~\ref{tab:cet_efficiency} rather than set aside.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Every headline number is generated from immutable, checksum-bound experiment outputs by a script that refuses to build a table from runs produced under superseded code, and the verdict sentences in table captions are derived from the same ledgers as the tables. The claim--evidence matrix records outcomes against the evidence each claim required, including the rows still marked refuted. Failed runs, both defects and their corrections, and the results on which the method does not help all remain visible.
