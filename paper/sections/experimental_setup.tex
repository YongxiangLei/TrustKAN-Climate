\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Datasets}
The results reported in this paper come from one observational source: a daily Central England Temperature (CET) station series, which provides continuity with prior climate KAN work and a long, clean, gap-free univariate history. The frozen panel spans \cetFirstYear{}--\cetLastYear{}, giving \cetSamples{} daily forecast origins after windowing, of which \cetTestSamples{} form the held-out test region covering \cetTestStart{} to \cetTestEnd{}.

Three further protocols were frozen in advance and are not reported, and we state the reason for each rather than presenting a four-dataset design the evidence does not support. The GHCN-Daily protocol is fully specified and its panel is prepared: one objectively selected station from each of \ghcnStations{} geographic strata, targeting daily midrange temperature from quality-controlled TMAX and TMIN over \ghcnFirstYear{}--\ghcnLastYear{}, with the panel fixed before any model comparison and its \ghcnObservations{} paired daily observations audited to the checksums recorded with it. Its campaign was planned but not executed within the compute budget available for this study, so no station-generalization result is claimed. The Jena protocol is likewise frozen in full, aggregating official 10-minute Beutenberg archives to hourly means of air temperature, pressure, relative humidity and wind speed over 2010--2020 and excluding derived thermodynamic variables; its runners, planner and audit are implemented, but the official archives were not retrieved. ERA5 region, variables and years remain to be frozen. Because each protocol was fixed independently of any observed model ranking, adding results later cannot constitute a post-hoc selection, and the conclusions here are correspondingly scoped to a single station.

\subsection{Chronological Splitting and Leakage Prevention}
All time series are split chronologically into training, validation, calibration, and final test regions. The GHCN fractions are 60\%, 15\%, 10\%, and 15\%, respectively. Normalization parameters are estimated from training observations only. Windowed samples are assigned according to forecast-target origin, and windows whose forecast target crosses a split boundary are excluded. GHCN windows must also be strictly daily-contiguous; missing days are not interpolated. Jena windows must be strictly hourly-contiguous after the frozen 10-minute quality-control and aggregation step. Hyperparameter selection uses training and validation data; conformal correction and reliability thresholds use calibration data; the final test set is reserved exclusively for evaluation.

\subsection{Forecasting Tasks}
Experiments include multiple forecast horizons selected according to each dataset's temporal resolution. The CET benchmark uses 365 daily history steps and 1-, 7-, 30-, and 90-day horizons. The frozen GHCN protocol uses 30 daily history steps and 1-, 7-, and 30-day horizons. The frozen but unreported Jena protocol uses 168 hourly history steps and 1-, 6-, and 24-hour horizons; equivalent horizon sets for ERA5 will be specified before its experiments.

\subsection{The Frozen GHCN Generalization Protocol}
\label{sec:ghcn_protocol}
This subsection specifies a design that was frozen and not executed. It is recorded because fixing it before any model comparison is what would prevent a later execution from being a post-hoc selection; no quantity in this paper comes from it, and no station-generalization claim is made anywhere in what follows.

As specified, GHCN evaluation would separate within-station temporal generalization from leave-one-region-out parameter transfer. In the transfer task, training and validation windows are pooled from four source regions and the fifth station is held out from model fitting and early stopping. Source regions are balanced by deterministic, chronologically uniform subsampling to the smallest source-window count in each split. Each station is standardized using only its own historical training period, so the transfer task permits target-history normalization but never target validation or test fitting. Immediately preceding target-station observations may form forecast inputs because they are available at prediction time. The protocol requires per-region reporting alongside equal-region macro averages, and forbids pooling forecast origins across stations as independent replicates.

\subsection{Baselines}
The frozen comparison set is ten models: a persistence rule, two classical machine-learning baselines (support vector regression and a random forest), a multilayer perceptron, LSTM and GRU recurrent networks, a temporal convolutional network, a Transformer, a standard KAN baseline, and the proposed \method. It was fixed before execution and is reported in full; a model that failed or was unavailable would be reported as such rather than dropped, and none was. Tuning effort is comparable across the learned baselines: the two classical models select hyperparameters from a declared candidate grid on the validation partition, and the neural models share one training recipe. A state-space (Mamba) forecaster and a Tem$^2$-KAN reference implementation exist in the codebase but are outside this frozen set, so neither is reported here.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Deterministic forecasting is evaluated using MAE and RMSE in physical target units. Probabilistic forecasting uses empirical interval coverage, mean prediction-interval width, and interval/calibration scores where supported. Reliability is evaluated using reliability--error correlation, large-error detection AUROC/AUPRC, risk--coverage curves, AURC, retained-sample error, and abstention rate. Efficiency analysis reports parameter count, training time, inference latency, and memory where measurable.

The 0.05 and 0.95 \method{} quantiles are calibrated to nominal 90\% coverage on the calibration segment. Horizonwise split conformal estimates a separate radius for each lead and targets marginal coverage; a second construction calibrates the maximum lead-wise conformity score per origin and targets simultaneous trajectory coverage. Because these are distinct estimands we report lead-wise and marginal coverage, joint coverage, mean width, and Winkler interval score for each rather than a single coverage figure.

Reliability combines interval-width and representation-shift percentiles through the geometric mean of Eq.~\eqref{eq:fusion}, whose weight is selected on the calibration partition by Eq.~\eqref{eq:weightselect}. The selection procedure, its grid, and its objective are frozen in advance; the resulting weight is data-dependent and recorded per run. The shift reference contains only training embeddings; empirical score mappings and abstention thresholds use calibration data. Risk--coverage ranking retains complete forecast origins rather than treating horizon elements as independent. Width-only and shift-only scores are evaluated as reliability ablations. Under the frozen GHCN transfer protocol of Section~\ref{sec:ghcn_protocol}, model parameters would remain source-only while the held-out station supplied its own earlier calibration segment, so any uncertainty result from it would have to be described as target-calibrated transfer.

Neural publication runs use an explicitly recorded CUDA device with deterministic PyTorch algorithms, deterministic cuDNN behavior, disabled cuDNN benchmarking, and automatic mixed precision disabled, and a seeded forward/backward replay must pass exactly before a campaign begins. Every run records its Python, library, CUDA, cuDNN, device, and memory metadata, and classical CPU shards are kept separate from neural GPU shards; the classical baselines in this study ran on CPU, which is why Table~\ref{tab:cet_efficiency} reports wall-clock cost only for the models that shared one accelerator. Hardware and software versions quoted anywhere in this paper are read from the environment artifact each run stores rather than transcribed.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
Headline neural comparisons use at least five independent random seeds. Paired comparisons are computed from predictions at identical forecast origins and timestamps. Because adjacent and multi-horizon windows overlap, primary confidence intervals for MAE and RMSE differences use a circular moving-block bootstrap over forecast origins, keeping all horizon elements from each origin together. Block length is fixed before final comparison and is at least the forecast-horizon width. Origin-level Wilcoxon tests and paired standardized mean differences are reported only as sensitivity analyses because they do not model serial dependence. Holm correction is applied to each pre-specified primary comparison family; exploratory families may additionally report Benjamini--Hochberg correction. Statistical evidence is interpreted together with effect magnitude and practical relevance rather than as a binary criterion.

\subsection{Ablation Design}
Each claimed component is tied to a removable module and a predefined failure criterion. A0 is the full model. A1 replaces the KAN mapping with a two-layer MLP whose width is chosen so that the parameter budget matches the KAN layer to within one percent, so the comparison isolates the mapping rather than capacity. A2 removes the quantile head; its degenerate quantiles reduce split conformal exactly to symmetric absolute-residual intervals, and its pinball weight is zero so no quantile gradient reaches the point head. A3--A7 are evaluation-side removals recomputed from the A0 artifact: conformal correction, the representation-shift score, the interval-width score, their fusion, and abstention. A8 replays the A0 predictions through sequential adaptive conformal and compares rolling-coverage deviation against the static construction.

Three further trained variants isolate the readout and the stem, which is necessary because \eqref{eq:dilated} and \eqref{eq:readout} were introduced together and would otherwise be credited as one change. A9 pools encoder states by a global average, A11 reads the final state alone, and A10 substitutes the symmetrically padded local stem while keeping the attention readout. A1, A9, A10, and A11 therefore each remove exactly one element of the design, and Section~\ref{sec:results_ablation} charges each for its own contribution. Ablations share the CET protocol, seeds, and forecast origins with the headline runs.

\subsection{Design Provenance}
\label{sec:correction}
Neither \eqref{eq:dilated} nor \eqref{eq:readout} was in the first version of this architecture, and we state how they arrived because the route is part of the evidence. An earlier iteration paired the symmetrically padded local stem with a mean-pooled readout. Under the audit described above it was beaten by a persistence baseline, and the receptive-field measurement of Section~\ref{sec:results_receptive} localized why: the readout discarded recency while the stem could not supply anything else, since only its last few positions were reachable. Both elements were replaced, and both are retained as trained ablations---A9 for the mean-pooled readout, A10 for the local stem---so that the superseded behaviour stays measurable rather than merely described.

Because the provenance hash covers the whole source tree, every change of this kind invalidates existing run records and forces re-execution; the audit refuses to aggregate across fingerprints so that the cost cannot be avoided by mixing campaigns. The baselines reproduce bit-identically under the new fingerprint, confirming that the architectural changes are isolated to the proposed model and that training remained deterministic. The evaluation protocol---splits, seeds, horizons, metrics, calibration policy, and statistical procedure---was not altered at any point, and no candidate readout or stem was chosen by comparing test performance.

\subsection{Robustness and Shift Protocols}
Robustness experiments use a pre-registered grid of standardized Gaussian noise, random history dropout, and recent-block dropout. Extreme-event subsets are labelled from training-period 5\%/95\% target quantiles on the first forecast lead; subsets with fewer than 30 origins are reported as underpowered. Adaptive/rolling conformal experiments evaluate coverage through time rather than only as an aggregate. Corruption levels and extreme definitions are not revised after examining final model rankings.
