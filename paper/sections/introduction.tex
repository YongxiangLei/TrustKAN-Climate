\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Data-driven climate and weather forecasting has benefited from rapid progress in deep temporal models, including recurrent networks, attention-based architectures, state-space models, and more recently Kolmogorov--Arnold Networks (KANs). Alongside this progress, a second literature has grown around trustworthiness: models that emit predictive intervals, monitor distribution shift, and abstain from forecasts they judge unreliable. The two literatures are frequently combined, and the combination is often evaluated as a whole. A system that contains conformal calibration, a shift detector, and an abstention rule is reported as trustworthy, with the reliability machinery credited to the architecture that houses it.

This paper reports what happened when we subjected such a design to a pre-registered evaluation of its individual parts. \method{} couples a temporal KAN representation with point and quantile prediction, split-conformal calibration on a dedicated partition, an embedding-based shift score, and a fused reliability score used for selective forecasting. It is, by construction, a representative instance of the pattern described above, and we built it intending to demonstrate that the pattern works.

Before running the headline experiments, we fixed the elements that would otherwise permit a favourable result to be assembled after the fact. Datasets, chronological splits, forecast horizons, random seeds, the family of statistical comparisons, and the calibration constants (nominal level, quantile grid, and fusion weights) were frozen and hashed. Every candidate claim was written into a claim--evidence matrix together with the specific artifact that would be required to support it, and each experiment records the checksums of its configuration, code, and data, so that a result computed under superseded code cannot silently survive into a table.

The outcome is negative and close to unanimous. On the Central England Temperature record at horizons of 1, 7, 30, and 90 days, the architecture wins none of forty pre-specified paired comparisons. A budget-matched plain KAN is significantly better at every horizon beyond one day, which is the ablation that was supposed to establish the temporal KAN module. Conformal marginal coverage is on target, but a split-conformal wrapper guarantees this for any backbone, so it evidences the wrapper rather than the method; meanwhile the joint coverage that a practitioner would actually rely on across a forecast trajectory collapses as lead time grows. The reliability score is outperformed by its own interval-width component at every horizon, and it ranks high-error forecasts at chance, so the abstention rule that depends on it recovers almost nothing.

We report this rather than repair it. The frozen fusion weights could be retuned until the selective-prediction claim passed, and a different backbone or horizon set could be substituted until an accuracy claim appeared; both moves are available and neither would be visible in a conventional results table. The pre-registration exists precisely to make such moves either impossible or disclosed, and its value is demonstrated better by a refutation than by a confirmation.

The contributions are therefore evidential and methodological rather than architectural:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We give a component-wise refutation of a representative trustworthy-forecasting design, in which the architecture, the fused reliability score, and the abstention rule each fail against the ablation that was pre-specified to test them, while the calibration wrapper succeeds for reasons that are independent of the method.
    \item We show that the fused score is not merely uninformative but actively harmful: combining interval width with an embedding-shift signal at the frozen equal weighting is worse at every horizon than using interval width alone, so the fusion degrades a component that works.
    \item We show through a budget-matched ablation that the KAN mapping itself is dispensable: replacing it with a multilayer perceptron of equal parameter count changes RMSE by at most $0.008$\,\degC{} at any horizon, which is inside the across-seed spread.
    \item We isolate the mechanisms that survive, both of which belong to the conformal wrapper rather than to the proposed method. Selective forecasting driven by interval width alone reduces error over no abstention by $2.2\%$, $7.1\%$, $9.5\%$, and $9.9\%$ at the four horizons, and adaptive conformal calibration reduces rolling coverage deviation by $13.7\%$ to $32.9\%$ for a width cost under $5\%$, with both benefits growing as forecasts become harder.
    \item We document an architectural defect discovered mid-study, in which a mean-pooled temporal readout discarded recency and left the model behind a persistence baseline at most horizons, together with the audit that exposed it and the re-execution it forced. The superseded behaviour is retained as an ablation so that the defect remains reproducible.
    \item We describe the reproducibility apparatus that made these conclusions binding: checksum-bound artifacts, a claim--evidence matrix that is updated against outcomes rather than intentions, and campaign auditing that refuses to aggregate runs produced under superseded code.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:experiments} defines the datasets, the frozen protocol, and the pre-specified comparison family. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the empirical outcome, followed by discussion and limitations in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
